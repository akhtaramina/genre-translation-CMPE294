# The Software Said "Maybe." Police Heard "Guilty."

**Live site:** https://akhtaramina.github.io/genre-translation-CMPE294/

An interactive online news article about why police treat facial recognition matches as evidence, even when written rules call them "investigative leads only." It follows the wrongful arrest of Christopher Gatlin in St. Louis.

This is a genre translation: it turns my literature review for an academic audience into a news article for everyday readers.

**Course:** CMPE 294, San José State University, Fall 2026
**Author:** Amina Akhtar

## What's on the page
- A real case and a timeline of documented wrongful arrests
- How a facial recognition match is made, step by step
- An interactive activity: choose a face from a candidate list
- A database-size graphic showing how look-alikes add up
- What the research counts, and what it leaves out
- Why the rule fails, and what would fix it

All sources are cited in APA style in the References section. Image credits are in the Image Links section.
